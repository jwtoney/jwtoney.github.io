I'm a PhD candidate in Chemical Engineering and Computational Science in the [Kulik Group](https://hjkgrp.mit.edu/) at MIT, where I work at the intersection of **machine learning and quantum chemistry**. My work is focused on transition metal complexes, notoriously challenging systems where conventional physics-based and data-driven methods fail, but are promising candidates for catalysis, sustainability, and medicine.

Specifically, I work on geometric deep learning models to predict the structural, chemical, and electronic properties of organometallics, including their bonding behavior, transition states, and quantum mechanical observables. I've worked to curate **[BOS-TMC](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jcim.6c01792)**, the largest and most diverse quantum chemical dataset of experimentally-synthesized transition metal complexes. Recently, I've been developing **[ElemeNet](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2606.30961)**, an open-source software package for molecular machine learning defined across the periodic table, and devising novel molecular fingerprints based on spectral graph theory. I enjoy merging electronic structure theory, machine learning, and high-performance computing to build new methods and accelerate scientific discovery!

I'm spending summer 2026 at **IBM Research**, working on wavefunction embedding methods for simulating large chemical systems on quantum computers, alongside generative models for protein structure prediction. Before MIT, I completed my B.S. and M.S. in Chemical Engineering at the University of Southern California, completing computational catalysis research with Professor Shaama Sharada, and spent a gap year as an Operations Engineer at **SpaceX** optimizing spacecraft production.

Outside of research, I enjoy concerts, writing music, old movies, dinosaurs, baseball, and Formula 1.

I'm open to work — if you're researching machine learning, computational chemistry, scientific computing, or anything in between, I'd love to chat!
