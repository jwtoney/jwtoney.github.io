I'm a PhD candidate in Chemical Engineering and Computational Science in the [Kulik Group](https://hjkgrp.mit.edu/) at MIT, where I work at the intersection of **machine learning and quantum chemistry**. My work is focused on transition metal complexes, notoriously challenging systems where conventional physics-based and data-driven methods fail, but are promising candidates for catalysis, sustainability, and medicine.

Specifically, I develop geometric deep learning models to predict the structural, chemical, and electronic properties of organometallics, including their bonding behavior, transition states, and quantum mechanical observables. I've worked to curate **[BOS-TMC](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jcim.6c01792)**, the largest quantum chemical dataset of experimentally-synthesized transition metal complexes. Recently, I developed **[ElemeNet](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jcim.6c02178)**, an open-source software package for molecular machine learning defined across the periodic table, as well as novel [molecular fingerprints](https://doi.org/10.1039/d6sc06309h) based on spectral graph theory. I enjoy merging quantum chemistry, machine learning, and computational science to build new methods and accelerate scientific discovery!

I spent last summer at **IBM Research**, working on wavefunction embedding methods for simulating large chemical systems on quantum computers, alongside generative models for protein structure prediction. Before MIT, I completed my B.S. and M.S. in Chemical Engineering at the University of Southern California, completing computational catalysis research with Professor Shaama Sharada. I subsequently spent a gap year as an Operations Engineer at **SpaceX** optimizing spacecraft production.

Outside of research, I enjoy concerts, writing music, old movies, dinosaurs, baseball, and Formula 1.

I'm graduating in summer 2027, and am open to work! If you're researching machine learning, computational chemistry, scientific computing, or anything in between, I'd love to chat!
